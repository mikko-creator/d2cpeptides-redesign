Keeping peptides cool in Fountain Hills

The town runs a little cooler than central Phoenix, though not by much. The Fountain Hills weather station in NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals stands at 481.6 meters, about 1,580 feet.9 Its July normals are a high of 103.0 °F and a low of 80.3 °F, against 106.5 °F and 84.5 °F at Phoenix Sky Harbor.910

That is still a desert summer. Bachem notes that freeze-dried peptides may be shipped at room temperature,7 but a July afternoon here, with a normal high of 103.0 °F, runs far above room temperature. Bring a delivered parcel indoors promptly rather than leaving it on a step or in a mailbox.

Once the parcel is inside, peptide makers' general guidance points to cold storage. Bachem advises keeping freeze-dried peptide in a tightly closed container below -15 °C (5 °F) for longer storage, and colder still, such as -50 °C (-58 °F) or below, for the long term.7 GenScript gives -20 °C (-4 °F), away from bright light.8 It says most freeze-dried peptides stay stable for several years that way, though stability depends on each sequence.8 The storage guide puts these steps in order.

About Fountain Hills

McCulloch Properties formally established Fountain Hills as a master-planned community in December 1970, and it incorporated as a town in 1989.121 The census counted 20,235 residents in 2000 and 22,489 in 2010.1314 Growth has since leveled off: the July 2025 estimate of 23,625 is slightly below the April 2020 base of 23,822.15

Scottsdale and the McDowell Mountains, which rise to 3,500 feet, lie to the west. The Salt River Pima-Maricopa Indian Community lies to the south, the Fort McDowell Yavapai Nation to the east and McDowell Mountain Regional Park to the north.12 The Census Bureau measures 20.28 square miles of land inside town limits, while the town cites 24 square miles.161 The town gives its elevation as 1,520 feet at the fountain and 3,000 feet on Golden Eagle Boulevard.1

Robert McCulloch built the town's fountain in 1970.12 It sprays for about 15 minutes at the top of every hour, reaching about 300 feet on its usual two pumps and up to 560 feet with all three.12 In January 2018, DarkSky International named Fountain Hills the world's 17th International Dark Sky Community, one of only two near a major metropolitan area.17 The town updated its outdoor lighting and sign ordinances as part of the effort.17 McDowell Mountain Regional Park, a county park on the town's north side, covers 21,099 acres in the lower Verde River basin, with more than 50 miles of multi-use trails.18

The population is far older than the county's. The 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey puts the town's median age at 61.5 years, against 37.6 for Maricopa County, and 40.7 percent of residents are 65 or older.192021 Half of adults 25 and older (50.4 percent) hold at least a bachelor's degree.2221

For medical care, the town's 2024 community profile lists the Fountain Hills Medical Center, with 24-hour emergency room services, plus HonorHealth urgent care and primary care.1 Next door in Scottsdale, the town's western neighbor, Mayo Clinic's Scottsdale location houses the Mayo Clinic Alix School of Medicine.23 It also has two research buildings, the Collaborative Research Building and the Samuel C. Johnson Research Building.23 Mayo names the focus areas of its Arizona research as blood cancers, diabetes, obesity and other metabolic and endocrine diseases, genomics, bioinformatics and aerospace medicine.24

Nearby

Scottsdale is next door. Its Census center point is about 9 miles from Fountain Hills', and the town's profile puts the drive at about 15 minutes.161 The same profile gives drives of about 27 minutes to Mesa, 28 minutes to Phoenix Sky Harbor Airport and 37 minutes to Chandler.1 In a straight line, the Sky Harbor weather station lies about 20 miles to the southwest.1610 Peptides in Phoenix is the hub for Maricopa County, and the locations page lists the Nevada and California pages as well.
