Keeping peptides cool in Glendale

Glendale's reference station is NOAA's Youngtown site, at about 1,135 feet and some 7.7 miles northwest of the city's Census center point.42 Its 1991 to 2020 normals give July a 104.9 °F high and a 79.7 °F low, and January a 65.4 °F high and a 41.1 °F low.4 The station averages 115.3 days a year at or above 100 °F and 175.4 at or above 90 °F, nearly half the year.1 It sees only 3.9 nights a year at or below freezing, and the June to August normal high is 103.5 °F.1

Those 115.3 days top the 111.3 recorded at Phoenix Sky Harbor,3 even though the July normal highs are close, 104.9 °F against 106.5 °F, and Youngtown's July normal low is almost 5 °F cooler.45

For long-term storage, peptide makers point to the freezer: GenScript, a peptide maker, gives -20 °C for freeze-dried peptides.6 How that freezer is used matters too. GenScript warns that peptides which go through freeze-thaw cycles are susceptible to degradation, and that splitting a peptide into separate vials, one per experiment, reduces both the number of cycles and its exposure to air.6 It also advises against opening the stock vial again and again.6 Peptides that contain cysteine, methionine or tryptophan oxidize easily, so GenScript recommends limiting air exposure and keeping vials tightly capped.6 The storage guide has more on freezer and fridge storage.

About Glendale

Glendale was founded in 1892 on farmland in the Salt River Valley and incorporated in 1910, when it had about 1,000 residents.7 The 2020 Census counted 248,325 people,8 and the Census Bureau's July 1, 2025 estimate is 260,572, up from 256,776 a year earlier.9 The city covers 67.3 square miles of land, about 1,143 feet above sea level at its Census center point.210 Its median age, 34.6, is three years below the county's 37.6 (American Community Survey, 2020 to 2024).11

The city's 6-square-mile Sports and Entertainment District is home to the NFL's Arizona Cardinals, the spring training facilities of the Los Angeles Dodgers and Chicago White Sox, and the Fiesta Bowl.12 The city-owned Desert Diamond Arena anchors the Westgate Entertainment District, and State Farm Stadium has hosted Super Bowls and the NCAA men's Final Four.12

The city counts about 12,400 health care workers.12 Midwestern University's 156-acre Glendale campus enrolls 3,833 students in eight colleges, among them the Arizona College of Osteopathic Medicine, the College of Pharmacy and the College of Veterinary Medicine.13 Phoenix Children's opened a 175,000 square foot Arrowhead Campus in the city, with inpatient care, an emergency department, surgery and advanced imaging.12 ASU's West Valley campus uses a Glendale mailing address, though ASU places it in northwest Phoenix near the city line.14
