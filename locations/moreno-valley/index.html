Keeping peptides cool in Moreno Valley

Redlands, at 1,410 feet, is the nearest NOAA normals station, about 7.9 miles north of the city's center point.678 August there averages a high of 96.2 °F and a low of 62.9 °F, and December a high of 66.5 °F and a low of 40.1 °F.6 A typical year brings about 104 days at 90 °F or more, 26 days at 100 °F or more and 3.3 nights at or below freezing, with 12.87 inches of precipitation.2 The Riverside Citrus Experiment station, about 9.4 miles west-northwest, logs a similar 24.1 days a year at 100 °F or more, so the heat is not a quirk of one station.47

Heat is not the only exposure to manage. GenScript, a peptide maker, advises storing freeze-dried peptides at -20 °C away from bright light.10 For peptides that contain Cys, Met or Trp, which it calls prone to oxidation, it also advises limiting air exposure and keeping vials tightly capped.10 Bring a delivered parcel inside promptly and store each vial as its product page directs. About Moreno Valley

Moreno Valley had 214,263 residents on July 1, 2025. That puts it second among the county's 28 incorporated cities and 21st in California.1 Its 51.513 square miles of land took shape from three rural communities: Moreno, Sunnymead and Edgemont.711 Census ZIP Code Tabulation Areas 92551, 92553, 92555 and 92557 cover the city.3 Households average 3.79 people.9

The valley is named for Frank E. Brown, whose Bear Valley Land and Water Company, formed in 1883, contracted to supply water to the new communities of Moreno and Alessandro; moreno is Spanish for brown.11 After failed votes in 1968 and 1983, the city incorporated on December 3, 1984.11

March Field was built in 1918 as a military airfield on 640 acres. As March Air Force Base it grew past 7,000 acres, and in 1996 it became March Air Reserve Base.11 With 7,000 people in 2025, the base is the largest employer counted for the city.12

The county's public teaching hospital is here too. Riverside University Health System Medical Center opened its current building in March 1998: 520,000 square feet, a tertiary care Level I trauma center licensed for 439 beds, with adult, pediatric and neonatal intensive care units.13 It trains medical students from UC Riverside, Loma Linda University and Western University of Health Sciences, and runs residency and fellowship programs.13

Lake Perris State Recreation Area, opened in 1973, covers more than 8,800 acres off the Ramona Expressway.1114 Box Springs Mountain, with its large "M", dominates the city seal adopted in 1985.11
