Keeping peptides cool in Gilbert

This page uses NOAA's Chandler Heights station, about 1,425 feet above sea level and some 8 miles south-southeast of Gilbert's Census center point.87 Its 1991 to 2020 normals put July at a 104.2 °F high and a 78.5 °F low, and January at a 65.6 °F high and a 40.7 °F low, on about 12 to 15 years of records.8 In an average year the station logs 94.8 days at or above 100 °F, 165.4 at or above 90 °F and 10.5 nights at or below freezing.1

Nights are the clearest difference from central Phoenix. The Chandler Heights July low is 6.0 °F cooler than Sky Harbor's 84.5 °F, and its January low is 5.3 °F cooler.89 So a Gilbert parcel can face a 100 °F afternoon in summer and a near-freezing night in winter.

The heat calls for the usual step: bring the box inside soon after delivery and store each vial at the temperature on its label or product page. Cold calls for another: a vial that comes in cold, from a winter doorstep or a freezer, should warm up before it is opened. Peptides tend to be hygroscopic, meaning they absorb water from the air, and Bachem says absorbed moisture lowers the peptide content of the powder and may also lower its stability.3 Both Bachem and GenScript advise letting a cold vial warm to room temperature before opening it, taking out what is needed quickly and resealing it tightly.3410 The storage guide has more on freezer and fridge storage.

About Gilbert

The Census Bureau estimates 287,285 residents on July 1, 2025, up from an April 2020 estimates base of 267,875, on 68.8 square miles of land.27 Gilbert grew up around a railroad siding on land owned by William "Bobby" Gilbert, after the Arizona Eastern Railway sought right of way for a Phoenix to Florence line in 1902.11 It incorporated on July 6, 1920, and was known as the "Hay Capital of the World" until the late 1920s.11 It began to take its present shape in the 1970s, when the town council approved a strip annexation of 53 square miles of county land, although its population in 1970 was only 1,971.11 Census survey figures for 2024 give a median age of 35.5, with 28.1 percent of residents under 18.1213

Downtown, known locally as the Heritage District, has more than 30 restaurants, a color-changing water tower, a theater, a historical museum and a gallery.14 Park University and the University of Arizona operate there from one shared facility.14 The Riparian Preserve at Water Ranch was developed in 1999 on 110 acres.15 Seven recharge basins covering 70 of those acres are filled in rotation with treated effluent, which percolates into the aquifer and is stored there for later use.15 About 298 bird species have been identified there, and more than 4.5 miles of trails cross the preserve.15

The town's economic development office counts more than 2.2 million square feet of hospital and clinical research space, including Banner MD Anderson Cancer Center, the Ironwood Cancer Research Center, Mercy Gilbert Medical Center, Banner Gateway Medical Center and Gilbert Hospital.16 More than 650,000 square feet of medical space is under construction or planned, and Banner Gateway's licensed inpatient beds rose from 177 to 286 after its expansion.16

Frequently asked questions

Gilbert is a town, not a city. Does that change delivery?

No. The Census Bureau does classify Gilbert as a town rather than a city,2 but delivery depends only on the street address. Orders for Gilbert ship from the USA and Canada under the same shipping policy as orders across the United States. Product pages show strengths and stock before checkout, and the returns page explains what to do if a parcel arrives damaged or short.

Which lab supplies are listed with the HGH vial?

The HGH product page lists five: bacteriostatic water, a 3 mL Luer-Lok syringe, an 18G 1-inch needle, insulin syringes and alcohol swabs. HGH is labeled in international units, 10 IU per vial, so the units converter and the reconstitution calculator help with the concentration arithmetic for lab work. The reconstitution guide covers sterile technique.

Can a Gilbert parcel get too cold in winter?

Cold is the smaller risk for a freeze-dried vial. NOAA's Chandler Heights station near Gilbert averages 10.5 nights a year at or below freezing,1 but peptide makers recommend keeping the dry powder frozen anyway, below -15 °C in Bachem's guidance.3 The catch comes when a cold vial meets a warm kitchen: peptides pull water from the air, so let the vial reach room temperature before opening it.4 Mixed solutions are different: the labels of approved tesamorelin and hCG products say not to freeze them.56

Is there anywhere in Gilbert to collect an order?

No. D2C Peptides lists no location in Gilbert or elsewhere in the county, so this page shows no address. Gilbert orders travel by shipping straight to the delivery address. The county's other pages are linked from Peptides in Phoenix, and order questions can be sent through the contact page.
