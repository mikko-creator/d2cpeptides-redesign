Keeping peptides cool in Temecula

The nearest NOAA normals station is Fallbrook 5 NE, at 1,140 feet, about 4.9 miles southwest of the city's center point.678 Its normals give August an average high of 89.3 °F and low of 61.8 °F, and December a high of 67.1 °F and low of 46.1 °F.6 The station averages 60.3 days a year at 90 °F or more, 8.9 days at 100 °F or more and almost no freezing nights (0.2 a year), with 15.31 inches of precipitation.2 NOAA built this station's temperature normals on only 12 to 13 years of data, so treat them as approximate.62

For comparison, Riverside Municipal Airport averages 21.7 days a year at 100 °F or more.3 By that measure Temecula's summers are milder than Riverside's, though far from cool.

Three different products are searched here, and storage is set product by product. GenScript, a peptide maker, notes that each peptide's stability is unique and depends on its sequence, and it advises storing freeze-dried peptides at -20 °C away from bright light.9 Bachem typically ships its peptides at room temperature and prints the recommended long-term storage condition on the label.4 Once the parcel is inside, follow the handling and storage section on each product page rather than the weather. The storage guide gathers the general rules.

About Temecula

Temecula had 114,865 residents on July 1, 2025, up about 4.4% since 2020. That ranks it fifth among the county's 28 incorporated cities, just ahead of neighboring Murrieta.1 It covers 37.162 square miles of land.8 Census ZIP Code Tabulation Areas 92590, 92591 and 92592 cover nearly all of the city. A small edge of 92028, a large area that also takes in Fallbrook and Rainbow, falls inside city limits too.5

The city places itself about 55 miles northeast of San Diego and 80 miles southeast of Los Angeles.10 It incorporated on December 1, 1989.10 The name comes from the Luiseño word "Temecunga", built from "temet" (sun) and "-ngna" (place of).10

Temecula calls itself the Heart of Southern California Wine Country, with wineries on about 3,000 acres.1011 Old Town mixes historic buildings with more than 640 antique dealers, shops and restaurants.11 Just south of the city, the Pechanga Tribe runs the Pechanga Resort and Entertainment Center, and Lake Skinner hosts the annual Balloon and Wine Festival.11

Health and life-science firms are among the city's largest employers. In June 2025 Abbott Laboratories had 1,793 workers in Temecula, second only to the school district.10 Temecula Valley Hospital had 1,344, up from 650 in 2016.10 FFF Enterprises (700) and Millipore Sigma (370) are also on the city's list of large employers.10 Temecula Valley Hospital is a general acute care hospital with a basic emergency department and 140 licensed beds.12
