Keeping peptides cool in Hemet

NOAA's normals station named HEMET sits about 5.9 miles east of the city's Census internal point, at about 1,811 feet.139 The ground at that internal point is lower, about 1,537 feet.14 The station's July normal high is 95.6 °F and August's is 96.7 °F, but July nights fall to 64.7 °F, a swing of about 31 degrees.13 The SAN JACINTO station, about 4.5 miles north-northeast and the closer of the two, has nearly the same normals: a June to August average high of 93.4 °F and 39.4 days a year at 100 °F or more.159 Most of HEMET's 11.75 inches of normal yearly precipitation, about 8.5 inches, falls from December to March.313 With cool nights and hot afternoons, the risky stretch is the afternoon: a box delivered at midday and left out sits through the hottest hours.

Heat also matters at the bench, when a vial is mixed. Bachem, a peptide maker, notes that dissolving a peptide may take time, occasionally up to several hours.16 Sonication for several minutes in a water bath may help with larger particles, but Bachem says excessive warming of the sample should be avoided.16 Cold has its own quirk: the FDA label for ZOMACTON (somatropin), an approved freeze-dried product, says some cloudiness may occur after refrigeration. The label says to let the product warm to room temperature and not to use it if the cloudiness persists.17 The reconstitution guide gives the lab steps, and the storage guide gives the cold-storage rules.

About Hemet

Hemet had an estimated 93,649 residents on July 1, 2025, up about 4.3% from its 2020 estimates base of 89,796.1 Its 29.262 square miles are all land, with no water area.9 Riverside County's San Jacinto Valley Area Plan places the valley between the Lakeview Mountains to the northwest, the Dawson Mountains to the southwest and the San Jacinto Mountains along its northeastern flank.2 The valley floor holds Hemet, San Jacinto and the unincorporated East Hemet and Valle Vista areas.2

The Lake Hemet Company and the Hemet Land Company were formed on January 27, 1887.12 The Great Hemet Dam, built in the mountains from 1891 to 1895 to a height of 122.5 feet, supplied water to the new town.12 Residents voted to incorporate on January 11, 1910.12 Since 1923, the Ramona Pageant, an outdoor play based on Helen Hunt Jackson's novel Ramona, has been staged each spring by people from Hemet and San Jacinto, with brief breaks during the Depression and World War II.12 Hemet Ryan Airport occupies the site of the Ryan School of Aeronautics, which the city says trained about 6,000 fliers for the Army Air Force from 1940 to 1944.12 In the southwest of the county's San Jacinto Valley plan area, Diamond Valley Lake is the Metropolitan Water District's reservoir of about 800,000 acre-feet, and the plan names the City of Hemet as the lake's other neighboring jurisdiction besides the county.2

The city describes its economy as based mainly on serving the senior community, with financial and health care services alongside, while noting that it is also drawing younger families.12 Hemet Unified School District is the largest employer, with 3,393 employees, or 12.03% of employment in the city.7 Other names on the city's 2024 principal employers list include the County of Riverside, Walmart, Stater Bros, the City of Hemet, Forest River and McCrometer.7 About 21.8% of residents are 65 or older, and the median age is 39.8.18 About 44.1% of households receive Social Security income, and the mean commute is 36.4 minutes.19 The city is home to 6,480 civilian veterans, about 9% of civilian adults.20
