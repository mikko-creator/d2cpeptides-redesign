About Glow Peptide Stack

Glow is three research peptides freeze-dried together at a fixed 10 : 1 : 1 ratio by weight, so the powder is five sixths GHK-Cu.21 GHK, a tripeptide found naturally in human plasma, binds copper, and most authors attribute its effects to that binding.2 BPC-157 is a synthetic 15-amino-acid fragment of a gastric juice protein.314 TB-500 is a synthetic, acetylated piece of thymosin beta-4 (amino acids 17 to 23), which FDA says is not the same substance as thymosin beta-4.4

The Glow peptide for sale here is one 60 mg vial for laboratory research.21 Read What is Glow Peptide Stack? for the full evidence review, or compare single ingredients among our research peptides.

Research highlights

  • No study of the blend. PubMed searches on October 24, 2026 found no study testing Glow or any GHK-Cu, BPC-157 and TB-500 mix; the only papers naming all three were reviews.109
  • Two ingredients together, in rats. In a 2026 randomized study of 32 rats after Achilles tendon repair, TB-500 alone significantly raised maximum load to failure versus controls; combining it with BPC-157 added no benefit over either peptide alone.11
  • GHK-Cu gel, in people. In a 1994 randomized, placebo-controlled trial in people with diabetes, a GHK-Cu gel on foot ulcers reached 98.5% median closure versus 60.8% with vehicle gel.12 Controlled human trials of injected GHK-Cu are absent, a 2026 review notes.13
  • BPC-157 in people. A 2026 systematic review found three small studies with 30 participants in total and rated the evidence very low certainty.14
  • TB-500 in people. FDA found no published study in which TB-500 was given to humans.4
  • Animal safety signals. In 28-day studies reviewed by FDA, rats and dogs given BPC-157 into muscle showed altered clotting times and liver-related signals.3

Regulatory status

Glow is not an approved drug: FDA states that neither BPC-157 nor TB-500 is a component of an FDA-approved drug.34 All three (GHK-Cu for injectable routes) were once in FDA's compounding Category 2, for substances that may present significant safety risks; the nominators withdrew them, and FDA still lists safety concerns for each.15 For its July 2026 advisory committee meeting, FDA proposed keeping BPC-157 and TB-500 off the 503A Bulks List of substances compounding pharmacies may use.16 The committee voted 8 to 6 to recommend them, a nonbinding vote, and no proposed rule had appeared in the Federal Register by October 24, 2026.1718 FDA says a meeting before the end of February 2027 will discuss GHK-Cu for the same list.22 WADA's 2026 Prohibited List covers BPC-157 (S0) and TB-500 (S2).19 Health Canada names all three among unauthorized injectable peptides it has seized and says "research use only" labels do not make them legal.20

Handling and storage

Glow ships as one freeze-dried powder, so adding diluent reconstitutes all 60 mg at once.21 Researchers who buy glow peptide therefore work with a fixed ratio: any solution made from the vial carries 10 mg of GHK-Cu and 1 mg of TB-500 per 1 mg of BPC-157.21 Lab steps are in How to reconstitute a peptide vial, and the reconstitution calculator works out concentration from vial size and diluent volume.

Supplies listed with this vial, each sold separately: Bacteriostatic Water, the 3mL Syringe, Luer-Lok Tip, the 18G 1-Inch Needle, Insulin Syringes and Alcohol Swabs. The glow peptide price is for the vial alone.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I find glow peptide for sale?

On this page. D2C Peptides lists the Glow Peptide Stack as one 60 mg vial of freeze-dried powder holding 50 mg of GHK-Cu, 5 mg of BPC-157 and 5 mg of TB-500.21 It is sold for laboratory research use only, not for use in people. Shipping details are on the shipping page.

How much do glow peptide injections cost?

The Glow vial (60 mg of freeze-dried powder) is listed at $85. It is a research chemical, not a ready-to-use injection or a treatment. No FDA-approved Glow product exists: FDA states that neither BPC-157 nor TB-500 is a component of any FDA-approved drug.34 Supplies are sold separately.

What strengths does Glow come in?

One: 60 mg per vial, at a fixed 10 : 1 : 1 ratio by weight, because the three peptides share one powder.21 A study that needs a different balance can use single peptides instead. GHK-Cu, BPC-157 and TB-500 are listed separately, and the Wolverine Stack (10 mg (5 mg + 5 mg)) pairs BPC-157 and TB-500 without GHK-Cu.

How should the vial be stored when it arrives?

Keep it sealed, dry and frozen. No published stability study of this mix was found.9 FDA cites reports that freeze-dried BPC-157 keeps 3 weeks at room temperature but should be stored dry below -18°C long term, and that sealed TB-500 powder kept from moisture and light lasts a year at -20°C.34 Handling after mixing is in the storage guide.

Is there a certificate of analysis for Glow?

See our lab results and certificates of analysis page. Check which tests a certificate reports, not only purity. FDA found that most BPC-157 certificates list purity results only, although it treats endotoxin testing as a critical quality attribute for injectable materials.3 A representative TB-500 certificate it reviewed did not test assay, impurities, endotoxins or aggregates.4
