About KPV

KPV is a tripeptide, a chain of three amino acids: lysine, proline and valine.6 It is the tail end of alpha-MSH, a 13-amino-acid hormone, which is why PubChem files it as Msh (11-13).12 A 2010 review reports that, in lab and animal models, KPV keeps almost all of the anti-inflammatory capacity of alpha-MSH but none of its pigment action.11

Much of the research is in mice with colitis, where KPV enters gut and immune cells through PepT1, a transporter for small peptides.613 FDA's 2026 review found no clinical studies, case reports or human exposure data for KPV by any route.3 KPV for sale here is a 10 mg research vial; the selector shows the KPV price and stock. For mechanisms, side effects and the full source list, read What is KPV? before you buy KPV for a study.

Research highlights

  • Mouse colitis (2008, 10 mice per group): KPV in drinking water reduced two chemically induced forms of colitis, with lower pro-inflammatory cytokine expression.126
  • Recovery from colitis (2008, mice): treated mice recovered sooner and regained more weight, and all treated mice lacking working MC1 receptors survived.13
  • Nanoparticle delivery (2010, mice): KPV packed into colon-targeted nanoparticles matched free KPV's effect at a 12,000-fold lower concentration.14
  • Colon tumors (2016, mice): KPV prevented colitis-linked tumors in normal mice but had no effect in mice without PepT1.15
  • Brain injury (2013, mice): one injection after the injury gave smaller lesions and less nerve-cell death.16
  • Mouth sores after chemotherapy (2021, rats): a gum gel carrying KPV and the plant compound EGCG improved eating, weight recovery and tissue repair.17

Regulatory status

KPV is not an approved drug.3 FDA's review dated May 12, 2026 says it has no USP or NF monograph and is not a component of any FDA-approved drug, and a Drugs@FDA search on October 24, 2026 found no approved KPV product.318 RAPS reported that on July 23, 2026, FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted 8 to 6, with 1 abstention, to recommend adding BPC-157 and KPV to the 503A bulks list, against the advice of FDA staff.19 NCPA reported that the votes are not binding on FDA and that pharmacies may not compound KPV until final rulemaking is in place.20 USADA's guide to the 2027 WADA Prohibited List, in force from January 1, 2027, does not name KPV but says that list names peptides in general under class S0.21

Handling and storage

Solubility depends on the salt form. Citing vendor documents, FDA reports that KPV free base dissolves in water only up to 0.70 mg/mL, while KPV acetate dissolves at 5 mg/mL.3 The supplier does not name its form, though its listed formula and molar mass match the free base.92 At those limits, 10 mg would need about 14.3 mL of water as free base, or 2 mL as acetate, to dissolve fully (our arithmetic). Under acid, alkali and peroxide stress, KPV breaks down mainly into lys-pro-diketopiperazine.22

How should KPV be stored when it arrives?

Colder storage gives the longest figures. Citing a vendor's website, FDA's 2026 review reports freeze-dried KPV free base as stable for up to 3 years at -20 °C in a tightly closed container, 2 years at 4 °C and 3 months at 15 °C.3 No independent study of mixed KPV was found.
