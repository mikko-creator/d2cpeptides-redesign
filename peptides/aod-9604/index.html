About AOD-9604

AOD-9604 copies residues 177 to 191 from the tail of human growth hormone (hGH), with one tyrosine added at the front.1 Its two cysteines, at positions 7 and 14, are joined by a disulfide bond.4 The Melbourne company Metabolic Pharmaceuticals developed and patented the peptide in the late 1990s as a candidate obesity drug.17

In obese rats and mice it reduced weight gain.89 In people, the developer's largest trial (536 adults) found weight loss versus placebo too small to reach statistical significance, and the company ended obesity development in February 2007.7 FDA concluded in 2024 that evidence of effectiveness for obesity is lacking for any route.1

Research highlights

  • Obese rats (2000): 19 days of AOD-9604 by mouth cut weight gain by more than half (15.8 g against 35.6 g), with no loss of insulin sensitivity.8
  • Obese mice and cell tests (2001): 14 days of AOD-9604 or hGH reduced weight gain and raised fat oxidation; unlike hGH, AOD-9604 did not raise blood sugar, bind the hGH receptor or make receptor-bearing cells multiply.9
  • Rabbit knees (2015): in 32 rabbits with induced osteoarthritis, weekly joint injections improved cartilage repair, more so when combined with hyaluronic acid.10
  • 12-week trial (300 obese adults, capsules by mouth): all five dose groups lost more weight on average than placebo, but the best group fell short of significance overall (p=0.1), reaching it only in the female subgroup.67
  • 24-week OPTIONS trial (536 obese adults enrolled, tablets by mouth, with diet and exercise): weight loss beyond placebo was under 1 kg in every dose group at 12 and 24 weeks, too small to be statistically significant.7
  • Human safety (six developer-funded trials, 893 adults, by mouth or by vein): the sponsor's authors reported side effects similar to placebo, no change in IGF-1, no worsening of glucose tolerance and no antibodies in those tested.6 FDA's 2024 review raised concerns about cancers reported in the 12-week trial and found too little information to support long-term safety.1

Regulatory status

AOD-9604 is not an ingredient of any FDA-approved drug, and it has no USP or National Formulary monograph.1 In a 12 to 0 vote on December 4, 2024, FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee advised against placing it on the 503A Bulks List, FDA's list of bulk drug substances that can be used to compound drugs under section 503A.111 The 2026 WADA Prohibited List names it under S2.2.3 as a growth hormone fragment, prohibited in and out of competition.12 The vial is labeled: For research use only. Not for human or veterinary use.

Handling and storage

According to a supplier certificate cited by FDA, up to 2 mg of the free base dissolves in 1 mL of water, so each 1 mg needs at least 0.5 mL.1 FDA also notes that peptides like AOD-9604 can clump and degrade with changes in pH, temperature, concentration or impurities.1 Lab steps and concentration math are in How to reconstitute AOD-9604, general technique in How to reconstitute a peptide vial, and the reconstitution calculator does the division.
