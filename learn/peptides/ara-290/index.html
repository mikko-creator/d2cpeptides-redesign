What is ARA-290?

ARA-290 (cibinetide) is an experimental 11-amino-acid peptide copied from one face of erythropoietin (EPO), the hormone that drives red blood cell production.12 It was designed to keep EPO's tissue-protective signal without that red cell effect.2 It has been tested in people only in a few small, short studies and is not an approved medicine.34

What sources report

ARA-290 was engineered from the three-dimensional structure of EPO by Michael Brines, Anthony Cerami and colleagues, and described in PNAS in 2008.2 It copies the aqueous, or water-facing, surface of EPO's helix B.2 Its first amino acid, glutamine, closes into a ring called pyroglutamate, which gives the name pHBSP.2 Araim Pharmaceuticals of New York developed it as ARA 290 and sponsored its FDA orphan designations.412 For lab work, the compound is listed as ARA-290 for sale in a freeze-dried research vial.

How it is thought to work

The developers propose that ARA-290 acts on an innate repair receptor, a pairing of the EPO receptor with the beta-common receptor (CD131).5 Their 2016 review says this receptor is usually absent from normal tissue and is switched on locally by injury, low oxygen or metabolic stress.5 EMA's summary says that, once activated, the receptor blocks the production of inflammatory messengers (cytokines), and that these actions are expected to reduce inflammation.6

The evidence for that model points two ways. In a nerve-injury pain model, ARA-290 relieved pain from light touch or cold in rats and normal mice, but not in mice lacking the beta-common receptor.26 An independent 2018 biophysical study, however, found that the outer regions of the two receptors did not bind each other directly, with or without EPO or ARA-290, and noted that the model is controversial.27

The peptide leaves the blood fast: in people, its elimination half-life is about 20 minutes after an injection under the skin and about 2 minutes after an intravenous one.5 Its effects can outlast it, since in rats pain relief lasted as long as 15 weeks, even when treatment stopped after five doses.26

Most of these trials were funded by or run with the developer, whose officers co-authored the papers and owned stock.235 The corneal and skin nerve counts are surrogate markers from short, small trials, and no trial lasted beyond 12 weeks or reached phase 3.16173

Lab and animal findings

  • Rodent injury models (2008): the peptide, studied as pHBSP, gave benefit in models of stroke and of kidney injury after interrupted blood flow, sped wound healing and improved cognitive function.2
  • Diabetic rats (2016): 4 weeks of daily dosing lowered blood glucose after 3 and 4 weeks, cut HbA1c by about 20% and raised glucose-stimulated insulin release from their islets, without changing hematocrit.18
  • Aging rats (2022): treatment from 18 months of age for 15 months preserved heart pumping function, limited the rise in blood pressure and lowered frailty scores, but median lifespan and overall survival did not differ from saline (P = 0.182).28

These are animal results, not evidence of the same effects in people.

What has not been studied

No human trial has tested ARA-290 for wound, tendon, muscle or sports injury healing.16172 It has not been tested for weight loss: the diabetes trial measured safety, HbA1c and nerve symptoms, not weight.22 Human data on mood and thinking come from one single-dose study, and no trial has tested memory, focus or cognitive decline.242 There are no human data on aging.28 Related goal pages: Peptides and healing and recovery, Peptides and blood sugar, Peptides and focus and memory and Peptides and weight loss.

How it compares with EPO and other peptides

Epoetin alfa, an EPO medicine for anemia such as in chronic kidney failure, makes the bone marrow produce more red blood cells, and MedlinePlus lists risks of blood clots, stroke, serious heart problems and shorter survival in some people with cancer.29 ARA-290 was designed not to raise red blood cells, but its long-term heart and cancer-related safety has not been studied.519 WADA names carbamylated and asialo EPO as examples of innate repair receptor agonists, the class ARA-290 is described as belonging to.105

ARA-290 is a different molecule from BPC-157 and TB-500, which the 2026 case series gave together with it.25 See What is BPC-157? and What is TB-500?.

Side effects

Multi-dose safety data come from trials lasting 28 days to 12 weeks in groups of 9 to 64 people, so long-term safety is unknown.52317 No approved label exists, so there is no official list of interactions, contraindications or warnings.412

Mild and common events

  • Phase 2b sarcoidosis trial, registry results: headache in 8 of 46 people on ARA-290 and 0 of 16 on placebo, nausea in 5 of 46 against 0 of 16, and fatigue in 5 of 46 against 2 of 16.31 Injection-site pain affected 3 of 46 on ARA-290 and 4 of 16 on placebo.31
  • Macular edema trial, 12 weeks: all 9 people had at least one adverse event, none serious.23 The most frequent were raised triglycerides (5 of 9), headache (4 of 9), head cold (4 of 9), raised glucose (3 of 9) and nausea (2 of 9).23 Investigators judged headache, head cold and nausea potentially drug related.23
  • Type 2 diabetes trial, 28 days: event counts were similar between groups, with 54 mild, 9 moderate and 1 severe on ARA-290, against 61 mild and 5 moderate on placebo.22
  • Sarcoidosis trials of 22 and 38 people: the intravenous pilot recorded no adverse events.19 The 28-day trial had no serious events and no injection-site pain or irritation.16

Serious events

  • Phase 2b trial: serious events affected 2 of 16 people on 1 mg (fainting, headache, chest tightness, shortness of breath and small bowel inflammation were listed), 1 of 14 on 8 mg (suicidal thoughts), and none on 4 mg or placebo.31 The registry does not say whether these were drug related.31
  • Type 2 diabetes trial: 4 serious events occurred in the ARA-290 arm, and 2 were judged possibly related.22 One was worsening of borderline kidney function (creatinine 119 to 159 micromol/L) in a patient whose furosemide dose had been raised, and the drug was stopped.22 A fatal heart attack after severe cellulitis, 2 weeks after the last dose, was judged unrelated.22
  • Mood: the 2020 macular edema report notes that 1 person with past depression, out of over 200 treated with cibinetide, had suicidal thoughts while using it.23 That trial screened for suicidal thoughts and found none.23

Antibodies and blood counts

  • No antibodies against ARA-290 were detected in the 28-day sarcoidosis trial, the 28-day diabetes trial or the 12-week macular edema trial.162223
  • Hemoglobin did not change in the 4-week intravenous trial (8.9 to 8.7 mmol/L on ARA-290, 8.6 to 8.7 on placebo), and the diabetes trial found no clinically significant changes in blood counts.1922

Trial drug was a sterile preparation made for the studies, so these safety data do not describe research-market vials.22

Reconstitution and storage

ARA-290 has no approved label, so no official instructions exist for reconstituting it, storing it or judging its stability.412 In the lab, enzymes in blood plasma and serum broke ARA-290 down extensively, leaving one main fragment, PyrEQLERALN.11 Storage figures for the vial sold here come from the supplier and appear on its product page. General lab handling, from sterile technique to concentration math, is in How to reconstitute a peptide vial, the reconstitution calculator and How to store peptides.

Regulatory status

United States: ARA-290 is not an approved medicine and has no brand name or prescription label.412 FDA's orphan drug database lists four designations, all sponsored by Araim Pharmaceuticals and all marked "Not FDA Approved for Orphan Indication": delayed graft function after kidney transplant (September 18, 2009), neuropathic pain in sarcoidosis (September 13, 2011), sarcoidosis (June 13, 2016), and survival and function of pancreatic islets after transplant (June 13, 2016).1341214

Compounding: FDA's 503A list updated May 14, 2026 places cibinetide (ARA-290) in category 3, substances nominated without adequate support.9 FDA says category 3 substances are not eligible for the policy that applies to category 1, and that it would consider taking action against a compounder who uses them.33 ARA-290 is not on FDA's page of bulk substances that may present significant safety risks.30 In a March 2026 letter to a research-peptide seller, FDA said the firm's website showed its products were intended to be drugs for human use, despite "Research Use Only" labels.34

Europe: the European Commission granted orphan designations for sarcoidosis (EU/3/13/1191, 7 October 2013) and for preventing graft loss in pancreatic islet transplantation (EU/3/16/1721, 29 August 2016).68 Both showed the status "Positive" when checked in October 2026, and sponsorship moved to Araim Pharmaceuticals Europe Limited, Ireland, in April 2019.68 An orphan designation is not a marketing approval.6

Development and sport: ClinicalTrials.gov lists four ARA 290 studies, none in phase 3: two completed, one of unknown status, and one terminated because the study drug expired with no replacement available.320 No current statement from the developer about its plans was found for this page. Under the 2026 WADA List, its class, innate repair receptor agonists, is prohibited at all times (S2.1.5).106

Frequently asked questions

Is ARA-290 the same as cibinetide?

Yes. Cibinetide is the official nonproprietary name, listed as both the INN and the USAN, for the peptide developed under the code ARA 290.7 It is also called pHBSP, short for pyroglutamate helix B surface peptide, and PubChem files it as CID 91810664 with CAS number 1208243-50-8.12

Is ARA-290 FDA approved?

No. FDA's orphan drug database lists four orphan designations for it, covering sarcoidosis, neuropathic pain in sarcoidosis, delayed graft function after kidney transplant and pancreatic islet survival after transplant, and marks each one 'Not FDA Approved for Orphan Indication'.4121314 An orphan designation is not an approval. No ARA-290 trial has reached phase 3, and no prescription label exists.34

Does ARA-290 work for neuropathy or nerve pain?

The evidence is small and mixed. In 4-week sarcoidosis trials, symptom questionnaires improved more than on placebo (22 and 38 people), and corneal nerve fiber area rose at 4 mg a day (38 and 64 people).151617 Average pain intensity, however, improved about as much on placebo.151617 In a 48-person type 2 diabetes trial, neuropathy scores improved more than on placebo.2122 No larger or longer trial has confirmed these results.3

Does ARA-290 raise red blood cells like EPO?

It was designed not to. In the 2008 lab and animal studies that described it, the peptide did not stimulate red blood cell production.2 Diabetic rats given it daily for 4 weeks showed no change in hematocrit, and in a 4-week trial in people with sarcoidosis, hemoglobin stayed level against placebo.1819 Effects of longer use in people have not been studied.5

Is ARA-290 banned in sports?

It falls in a banned class. The 2026 WADA Prohibited List bans innate repair receptor agonists at all times, in and out of competition, under class S2.1.5, and the ban is not limited to the examples it names.10 EMA describes ARA-290 as activating the innate repair receptor, and anti-doping scientists in Cologne called it a prohibited peptide in 2017.611 Athletes can confirm its status with their anti-doping organization.
