Dosage information

DSIP dosage

No human dose of DSIP is established, and no approved label exists.1 Published human studies gave it intravenously, most often 25 or 30 nmol/kg, and FDA found no study of injection under the skin.12 The doses on this page come from those studies and from animal work, and they are not guidance for people.

No human dose is established

DSIP has no FDA-approved label, so there is no labeled dose, schedule or route.1 FDA evaluated a proposed 1 mg/mL injection under the skin, but found no study supporting effectiveness by that route and no safety data for it.1 Across published studies, FDA counted 209 people given intravenous doses of 25 to 150 nmol/kg, for 1 to 15 days.1 Every dose below comes from a published study and is listed for context only.

Doses used in human studies

Doses are written per kilogram of body weight. FDA's summary gives 25 nmol/kg as 21.23 micrograms per kilogram.1

How the injections were given

Speed of infusion mattered in the early work.171 Researchers reported in 1983 that slow injection proved essential, with sleep starting about 1 hour after injection.17 FDA's summary adds that, in healthy volunteers, DSIP was completely ineffective when infused over 1 minute, and total sleep time rose most with infusions over 4 to 8 minutes.1 DSIP also clears fast: FDA reports a plasma half-life of 8 minutes, with blood enzymes cutting it into shorter peptides.1

These details matter when reading claims about other routes. The studies that reported sleep effects used timed intravenous infusions in sleep laboratories. No study has tested whether those results hold for injection under the skin or for nasal sprays, and FDA found human data only for intravenous use.1

Doses used in animal studies

  • Rabbits, 1977: synthetic DSIP infused into the brain ventricles increased delta and spindle EEG patterns.18
  • Rats, intravenous: 30, 40 or 80 nmol/kg raised EEG delta-wave power between 1 and 4.5 hours after the injection.1
  • Rats, into the brain ventricles: 6 nmol/kg lengthened sleep, and the opioid blocker naloxone prevented that effect.1
  • Dogs, intravenous: a 100 micrograms/kg bolus was used to show DSIP passing into the cerebrospinal fluid.19

Animal doses are not human doses, and none of these studies was designed to find a dose for people.

Frequently asked questions

How long can I take DSIP for?

No safe duration has been established for anyone.1 Published human studies lasted 1 to 15 days, and FDA found no information on benefit with long-term use.1 FDA also calls safety for long-term intermittent use insufficiently characterized.1 In the early insomnia studies, effects were reported to build up, with sleep structure normalizing after four injections, but those studies were small and supervised.3

How to use DSIP spray?

No approved instructions exist for a DSIP nasal spray, and none of the studies reviewed here gives a nasal dose.1 FDA found US clinics and retailers marketing DSIP sprays, yet all the human data in its 2026 review came from intravenous use.1 The only nasal report found, a 2001 letter, has no published abstract, so its dose is not known.4 The DSIP listed in this store is a freeze-dried vial for lab research, not a spray.

Can DSIP be taken by mouth?

There is no evidence that it works by mouth.51 In a lab model of the human gut lining (Caco-2 cells), DSIP was broken down quickly, with 8.2% left after 2 hours, and none of it crossed the cell layer.5 No oral dose appears in the human studies FDA reviewed, which used intravenous injection only.1

Did higher doses of DSIP work better?

Not in a simple way.6 In rats, intravenous doses of 30, 40 and 80 nmol/kg raised delta-wave power, with larger effects at higher doses.1 But a 1984 review reports a U-shaped activity curve for both the dose and the infusion time, so more did not reliably mean more effect.6 In a 2009 trial in 12 women under isoflurane anesthesia, DSIP at 25 to 100 nmol/kg made anesthesia lighter, not deeper, as measured by the bispectral index.7
