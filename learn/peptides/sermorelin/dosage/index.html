Sermorelin dosage

No current human dose of sermorelin is established: its only approvals, a treatment for GH-deficient children and a one-time diagnostic test, were withdrawn in 2009.12 This page records the doses used in published human studies, with population, route and length. They are research history, not guidance for anyone.

No established human dose

FDA approved sermorelin (Geref) for only two uses: treating idiopathic GH deficiency in children with growth failure, and testing the pituitary's ability to secrete growth hormone.1 Both approvals were withdrawn effective June 18, 2009.1 Drugs@FDA offers no Geref label, and DailyMed returns no sermorelin labels, so there is no label dosing table to reproduce.910 A 2026 review notes that no current approval covers body-composition or performance use.2

No dose is established for adults.12 The doses below come from small, mostly short studies. They show what researchers gave to specific groups, and they are not guidance for anyone. For how dose units and study doses are read in general, see Peptide dosage basics. The compound itself is described in What is Sermorelin?

Doses used in published studies

All studies below were in humans. Doses written in mcg/kg scale with body weight; doses in mg were fixed amounts.

What the dose studies showed

  • Dose size mattered in older men. Only the 1 mg twice-daily dose significantly raised 24-hour GH and IGF-1; the 0.5 mg dose did not reach significance.5
  • Once-nightly dosing looked weaker. A single nightly 2 mg dose raised nighttime GH but not IGF-1, and the authors suggested single nightly doses are less effective than multiple daily doses.6
  • In one child trial, the higher daily dose did better. In the Neyzi trial, where both doses were split into three injections, 60 mcg/kg per day gave growth comparable to GH, while 30 mcg/kg per day gave the lowest growth.11
  • Continuous infusion fell short of GH. The Chen authors concluded that continuous GHRH infusion is unlikely to be as effective as GH for growth.7
  • The nasal route absorbed poorly. In healthy men, only 3 to 5% of a nasal dose reached the blood; in children the spray's effect faded, and the authors ruled the form unsuitable.814

These results describe study groups, measured with study methods. They do not convert into an amount for any person.

Routes and timing in the studies

Studies gave sermorelin under the skin, into a vein, by continuous infusion or as a nasal spray.4714 The peptide clears from the blood within minutes, with a disappearance half-time near 4.3 minutes according to a 2026 review.2 Even so, GH stayed raised for about 3 hours after one IV dose.8 Nasal absorption was only 3 to 5%, and about 50 mcg/kg in the nose gave the GH response of 1 mcg/kg into a vein.8

What the studies measured

Endpoints differed by group. Child trials measured height velocity over 6 to 12 months.37 Adult studies measured GH and IGF-1, and the 6-week study added strength tests and DEXA body composition.56 A 1999 review noted that the effect on final adult height had not been determined.4 No study here followed long-term outcomes, and no long-term safety data exist.2

Why study doses do not transfer

Study groups were selected. The children were GH-deficient, the 6-week study enrolled older men with low IGF-1, and the 14-day study enrolled nonobese men.365 Other factors changed the response in testing. In a GH test of 14 obese and 9 normal-weight women, the obese group peaked at a mean 8.9 mU/L against 28 mU/L, and in another study atropine cut the GH response.1617 So a dose used in one group cannot be assumed to apply to another.

How online regimens compare with study doses

A 2026 review set dosing regimens from fitness and bodybuilding websites beside the regimens used in peer-reviewed studies to show how widely they vary.2 The review adds that in unregulated supply chains, product content, dosing and stacking are all uncertain.18 Labeled strength is not always what a vial holds: compounded sermorelin has been recalled as out of specification, and the recall notice, posted by FDA, warns that such products can lead to too much or too little being given.19

Frequently asked questions

Is there a standard sermorelin dosage?

No. No dose is established for any person, and this page does not give one. Sermorelin's only FDA approvals, a treatment for children with GH deficiency and a one-time diagnostic test, were withdrawn in 2009.1 What exists is study history: the doses researchers gave to specific groups, listed in the table on this page with population, route and length. A 2026 review set regimens from fitness websites beside study regimens to show how widely they vary.2

Do you have to cycle off sermorelin?

Published studies did not test cycling schedules, so the sources reviewed here cannot answer this. The longest data come from children. During 6 months of GHRH(1-29)-NH2, IGF-1 rose at first and then fell back to about pretreatment values.7 Nearly all of those children formed GHRH antibodies, which had almost disappeared 9 months after treatment stopped.7

Is 3 months of Sermorelin enough?

No study reviewed here sets a course length for adults. Adult studies of sermorelin alone were short: 14 days at each of two doses in 10 older men, and 6 weeks of nightly injections in 11 older men.56 In GH-deficient children treated for 6 months, IGF-1 rose at first and then returned to about pretreatment values.7 Three months is longer than any adult study of sermorelin alone.

How long does it take for sermorelin to start working?

It depends on what is measured. After a single IV dose in healthy men, GH stayed raised for about 3 hours.8 In older men, 14 days on the higher tested dose raised GH and IGF-1, while 6 weeks of single nightly doses raised nighttime GH but not IGF-1.56 In GH-deficient children, faster growth was measured at 6 months.3
