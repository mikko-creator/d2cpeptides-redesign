Dosage information

Glow Peptide Stack dosage

No glow peptide dosage has been established. No study has tested the blend, and no human dose exists for injected GHK-Cu, BPC-157 or TB-500.12 This page lists doses used in published studies of each ingredient, with species, route and length. It also explains why none is a dose for people.

Is there a published Glow dose?

No. As of October 24, 2026, PubMed held no study of Glow, KLOW or any GHK-Cu, BPC-157 and TB-500 blend.13 The six papers naming all three ingredients were review articles.3 No study dose therefore exists for the blend. The amounts below come from studies of one ingredient at a time, or of a related molecule. Each row names who or what was studied, the route and the schedule.

Why these numbers are not a dose for people

The rows above used different molecules, routes, species and lengths. They cannot be combined into a Glow dose or scaled to a person.12 Several details make this concrete:

  • The human BPC-157 doses were exploratory. FDA says those studies were short, had small samples and evaluated doses that were likely exploratory in nature.4
  • The thymosin beta-4 trials did not use TB-500. FDA says thymosin beta-4 and TB-500 are not the same substance.6 It also says results for the closely related unacetylated sequence cannot be directly extrapolated to TB-500.6
  • The rat combination study used a different mix. It gave six times as much TB-500 as BPC-157 by body weight and no GHK-Cu.5 In Glow, BPC-157 and TB-500 are present in equal amounts and GHK-Cu makes up most of the powder; the contents are listed in What is Glow Peptide Stack?.
  • Glow's ratio is fixed. Any BPC-157 drawn from a Glow solution comes with the same amount of TB-500 and a much larger amount of GHK-Cu, so the blend cannot reproduce a study that gave one ingredient alone.
  • Human GHK-Cu data are topical. No controlled human trial has tested GHK-Cu by injection.16 No validated human safety threshold exists for GHK-Cu, on the skin or by injection.11
  • Labeled amounts may not match. A 2026 review reported significant deviations from stated doses when direct-to-consumer peptides were tested.16

How long the ingredients last in the body

No study has measured the blend in blood or tissue.1 The animal figures that exist describe short exposure:

  • BPC-157 given into a vein had a half-life of 5.3 minutes in dogs and 15.2 minutes in rats.4 FDA found no human pharmacokinetic data for BPC-157 given by mouth, under the skin, by nasal spray or on the skin.4
  • In horses, one 10 mg dose of TB-500 under the skin peaked in plasma at 0.05 to 0.08 ng/mL within 60 to 120 minutes. It could no longer be measured after 6 to 10 hours.6
  • For GHK-Cu, formal absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion studies of the intact complex have not been reported.11

These figures describe blood levels in animals. They do not show how long any effect lasts, in animals or in people.

Trials that may add human data

Four registered trials of single Glow ingredients had posted no results as of October 2026:

  • Topical GHK-Cu gel for acute skin wounds (recruiting).17
  • BPC-157 for acute hamstring strain, injected under the skin by trained study staff (recruiting).1718
  • TB-500, described as the 17 to 23 fragment of thymosin beta-4, and cardiovascular biomarkers in stable atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (recruiting).17
  • BPC-157 for recovery after rotator cuff repair surgery (not yet recruiting).17

Frequently asked questions

What is the recommended dosing protocol for glow peptide?

There is none. No study has tested the blend, so no protocol has a research basis.1 A 2026 review of injectable peptides says that indications, dosing, frequency and duration of treatment remain unknown.2 Weekly schedules labeled beginner or advanced on seller websites are not drawn from any published study of Glow, because no such study exists.1

How many times a week do I take glow peptide?

No schedule has been studied or established for Glow, and this page does not give one.1 Glow is sold for research use only. Schedules in single-ingredient studies vary too widely to transfer. A small human study used one or two knee injections of BPC-157, while a rat tendon study used daily abdominal injections for 4 weeks.45

How much glow peptide do you inject daily?

No daily amount is established, and none is given here. No published study has tested Glow in people.1 FDA's literature search found no article in which TB-500 was given to humans, and FDA calls its potential safety risks in humans unknown.6 Because the three peptides share one vial, any amount drawn from it is an untested mix of all three.

How long to take glow peptide?

No duration is established, because no study has given Glow for any length of time.1 The human BPC-157 studies FDA reviewed were short and small, with doses FDA calls likely exploratory.4 Topical GHK-Cu skin studies ran for 12 weeks, but they tested creams, not injections or the blend.19
