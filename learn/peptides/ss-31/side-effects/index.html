Side effects

SS-31 side effects

Injection site reactions are the most common reported side effect of SS-31 (elamipretide).1 In the Barth syndrome trial, all 12 patients had one on elamipretide, against 8 of 12 on placebo.2 The label also warns of serious allergic reactions, and carcinogenicity studies had not been done at approval.2

Very common: injection site reactions

In the Barth syndrome crossover trial, the label reports these reactions in 12 patients on each treatment:2

Reaction at the injection site Elamipretide Placebo
Any local reaction 100%2 67%2
Redness (erythema) 100%2 25%2
Pain 75%2 42%2
Hardening (induration) 67%2 17%2
Itching (pruritus) 67%2 17%2
Bruising 25%2 0%2
Hives (urticaria) 25%2 0%2

Other trials showed the same pattern. In a 30-adult mitochondrial myopathy trial, 80% had injection site reactions on elamipretide, mostly mild.7 In a 176-adult macular degeneration trial, 86% on elamipretide and 71% on placebo had an adverse event.8 Injection site itching, pain, bruising and redness were the most common.8 The label says such reactions can be treated with antihistamines or topical corticosteroids.2

Reactions after injection made 2 of 10 patients in the Barth syndrome extension stop for good.6 One had moderate hives at the injection site. The other had redness and hives on both sides of the abdomen, plus a rash on the opposite side that FDA reviewers thought was a whole-body (systemic) reaction; both events cleared the same day.6

Serious: allergic reactions

  • The label reports hypersensitivity reactions, including serious allergic reactions that needed emergency treatment.2
  • Signs have included rash, raised bumps, eczema-like skin inflammation and cough.29
  • They can begin within minutes of starting treatment or only months later.2
  • The label rules out use in anyone with a known serious allergy to elamipretide or any ingredient.2
  • After a serious reaction, the label says to stop the drug, give emergency care and never restart it.2

Lab changes: eosinophils

Counts of eosinophils, one kind of white blood cell, often rose in studies lasting 30 days or more.2 They peaked around day 90, with a mean rise of roughly 500 to 600 cells per microliter.2 Counts returned to baseline after 6 to 12 months of continued use or after stopping.2 The rise was not linked to symptoms or to changes in other lab values.2

Serious adverse events in trials

  • Barth syndrome extension: five serious adverse events in 3 patients, all related to infection or inflammation and judged unlikely to be caused by the drug.6
  • Mitochondrial myopathy: no serious adverse events or deaths in the 30-adult crossover trial.7 In the 218-adult phase 3 trial, most adverse events were mild to moderate.3
  • Heart failure: a single infusion in 36 adults caused no serious adverse events.10 In a 71-patient trial, drug-related adverse event rates were similar across groups.11
  • Eye drops: in 12 adults with Leber hereditary optic neuropathy, there were no serious adverse events.12
  • Across trials: a 2022 systematic review found no serious treatment-emergent adverse events with mitochondria-targeted antioxidants.13 Injected elamipretide did add mild to moderate injection site events.13

Label warnings and unknowns

  • Benzyl alcohol: Forzinity contains 20 mg per mL as a preservative.2 It is not approved for newborns, because benzyl alcohol can cause fatal toxicity in premature and low-birth-weight infants.2
  • Drug interactions: elamipretide blocks the kidney transporter MATE1 in lab tests, so FDA required a study of its effect on metformin.24 It did not change the effects of aspirin or clopidogrel.2
  • Heart rhythm: there was no clinically significant QT prolongation at 3 times the peak level of the top recommended dose.2
  • Cancer: risk is unknown, and FDA required animal carcinogenicity studies after approval.4
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding: there are no human data, because Barth syndrome is not likely to affect females.2 Animal reproduction and fertility studies found no harm at the doses tested.2
  • Age and size: safety is not established under 30 kg, and the Barth syndrome studies enrolled no one aged 65 or older.2
  • Overdose: none was reported in the trials, and the label expects histamine-type signs such as low blood pressure and feeling faint.2

Kidney function changes the label dose; details are on SS-31 dosage.

Research-grade SS-31

These findings come from clinical trials of elamipretide and from the label of the approved product.5 Research-grade SS-31 is not that product. FDA warns that injectable products go straight into the body, sometimes into the bloodstream, and bypass some of its defenses against toxins and microbes.14 FDA's August 2026 warning letters said online sellers' SS-31 was an unapproved new drug even with "research use only" labels.14 General safety points are in Peptide safety basics. Frequently asked questions

Is SS-31 peptide safe?

No source establishes the safety of research-grade SS-31; the safety data come from clinical trials of elamipretide and the label of the approved product.5 In a 218-adult phase 3 trial it was well tolerated, with most adverse events mild to moderate.3 Injection site reactions were very common, serious allergic reactions have occurred, and eosinophil counts often rose for a time.2 FDA required cancer studies only after approval.4

What are the side effects of SS-31?

Three kinds stand out in the Forzinity label. Injection site reactions (redness, pain, hardening, itching, bruising and hives) are the most common.2 Allergic reactions, including serious ones, can start minutes to months after treatment begins.2 Eosinophil counts often rise for several months without symptoms.2

How do the liver and kidneys handle SS-31?

Lab tests found no breakdown of elamipretide by the liver.2 The drug is cut into inactive fragments and cleared by the kidneys.26 Exposure more than doubled in adults with severe kidney impairment not on dialysis, and the label lowers their dose.2 The SS-31 dosage page lists the kidney doses.

Has SS-31 been tested for cancer risk?

Not fully. No carcinogenicity studies had been done when FDA approved Forzinity in 2025.2 FDA then required a 26-week study in mice, with a final report due in February 2026, and a 2-year study in rats, due to finish in October 2029.4 No results from either study appear in the sources we reviewed. Standard genetic toxicity tests were negative: a bacterial mutation test, a chromosome test in hamster cells and a rat bone marrow test.2 Long-term cancer risk in people is unknown.
