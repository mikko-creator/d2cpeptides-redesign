What a certificate of analysis is

A certificate of analysis (COA) is a test report for one batch, or lot, of a substance. FDA's Q7 guidance on making active pharmaceutical ingredients says a certificate should be issued for each batch on request, and sets out what it should carry:1

  • the name of the substance, its batch number and its date of release;
  • every test performed, each with its acceptance limit and its numerical result;
  • a date, and the signature of authorized staff of the quality unit; and
  • the name, address and phone number of the company that originally made it.

Use those four points as a checklist for any document a seller shows you. A report with no lot number, no numbers or no date tells you little about the vial in your hands.

The tests that matter for a peptide

A useful certificate for a research peptide answers four separate questions. Each needs its own method and its own result.

Question Common method What to look for
Identity: is it the right peptide? Mass spectrometry, which identifies a chemical by its mass.2 For peptides it can determine molecular mass and confirm the amino acid sequence.3 A measured mass that matches the compound's expected mass
Purity: how free is the sample of impurities? HPLC, a fast, high-sensitivity form of liquid chromatography4 A percentage, with the method named
Quantity: how much peptide is in the vial? Liquid chromatography coupled with mass spectrometry, as a 2024 study used to measure semaglutide in vials bought online5 A measured amount set against the label strength
Endotoxin: is there bacterial toxin? The LAL (Limulus amoebocyte lysate) test, which FDA has accepted for more than 30 years in place of the rabbit pyrogen test6 A numerical result with its limit

An endotoxin is a toxin closely bound to the cytoplasm or cell wall of certain microorganisms and released when the cells break apart.7 FDA guidance notes that the USP and AAMI standards recommend testing components and finished products for pyrogens and endotoxins.6

A peptide medicine is held to a longer list. The European Medicines Agency's guideline on synthetic peptides, in effect from June 1, 2026, lists identification, purity (total and individual impurities), assay or content, water content, residual solvents, elemental impurities, bacterial endotoxins and microbiological purity among the tests in a peptide's specification.3 Under the European Pharmacopoeia thresholds it cites, peptide-related impurities in a medicine should be reported above 0.1%, identified above 0.5% and qualified above 1.0%.3

A certificate may also name the salt form. EMA notes that synthetic peptides usually carry acetate as the counter ion, although trifluoroacetate (TFA) or chloride are possible.3

Why lot documents matter

Laboratory testing of peptides bought online has found wide gaps between label and contents. A 2024 study test-bought semaglutide from illegal online pharmacies and analyzed the 3 vials that arrived. It found endotoxin in every vial, semaglutide content 28.56% to 38.69% above the label, and measured purity of 7.7% to 14.37% against the 99% the labels claimed.5

A 2018 Belgian study screened the 10 most frequently encountered falsified peptide drugs, bought from three suspected illegal internet pharmacies. It found large variation in the amount of drug per unit, purity of 5% to 75% for cysteine-containing peptides, and arsenic and lead in some samples.8

A certificate describes one lot, and it does not change what a product is sold for. Each of our product pages carries the label: For research use only. Not for human or veterinary use. The research peptides overview explains what these compounds are and how US rules treat them.

How to match a certificate to your vial

  1. Find the lot number on the vial or its packaging, and record it with your study notes. If you cannot find one, go to step 5.
  2. Look for the same product and lot number among the certificates on this page.
  3. Check that the compound, the strength and the lot on the certificate match the vial exactly.
  4. Read which tests were run, the method for each, the numerical results and limits, the analysis date and the name of the lab.
  5. If your lot is missing, you cannot find a lot number, or any detail differs, write to support@d2cpeptides.com with the product and strength, and the lot number if you have it, before you open the vial.

Frequently asked questions

What does an HPLC purity figure on a certificate mean?

HPLC (high-performance liquid chromatography) is a liquid chromatography technique that uses high inlet pressure and offers high sensitivity and speed.4 A purity figure describes how free the sample is of impurities, and EMA lists purity, as total and individual impurities, among the tests in a peptide's specification.3 Identity is a separate test, often run by mass spectrometry, whose variants can measure a peptide's molecular mass and confirm its sequence of amino acids.3 So read the method next to every number, and look for an identity result as well as a purity figure.

Does a 99% purity figure mean the vial holds the labeled amount?

No. Purity and quantity are separate results. Purity describes how free the material is of impurities, while quantity is how much peptide the vial actually holds. A 2024 laboratory study of semaglutide vials bought online measured drug content with liquid chromatography coupled with mass spectrometry and reported it apart from purity: content ran above the label while purity measured far below the labels' claim.5 A certificate that gives only a purity figure leaves the amount in the vial unmeasured, so look for both.

Why is my lot number not listed?

Certificates are listed by product and lot only when they are published, so your lot may not have one here. Write to support@d2cpeptides.com with the product, the strength and the lot number from the vial or its packaging, and we will tell you what documentation exists for that lot. Do this before you open the vial or use it in a study.

Who tests the vials you sell?

This page names a testing laboratory only on a published certificate for a lot we sell, so that no lab is credited with a vial it did not test. Each certificate we publish will name the laboratory that ran the tests, the methods it used and the date of analysis. For a lot with no certificate listed here, write to us with the product and lot number.
